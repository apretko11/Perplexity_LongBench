The six heatmaps reinforce the finding that attention dropout's effectiveness depends strongly on model size, dropout configuration, and downstream task. Across the 16K-trained models, the 906M architecture shows the most consistently favorable results relative to the no-dropout baseline, particularly on retrieval and question-answering tasks. For example, standard attention dropout at 0.2 reduces gold-answer perplexity on Passage Retrieval by 71.1%, Musique by 33.6%, and TriviaQA by 33.7%. Several ILD configurations also produce substantial improvements for 906M, although the benefits vary across tasks and dropout rates. In contrast, the 271M and 504M architectures frequently experience large degradations on TREC and Passage Count, even when other tasks improve. Notably, the 504M models often benefit from standard attention dropout on Passage Retrieval but can deteriorate substantially with ILD, indicating that dropout placement matters alongside dropout probability. Overall, these results suggest that moderate attention dropout can improve long-context reference-answer perplexity, particularly for the larger architecture, but neither higher dropout rates nor ILD consistently outperform standard attention dropout. All comparisons use the same zero-dropout baseline within each model size, with five LongBench examples per task; the findings should therefore be treated as descriptive rather than definitive evidence of improved downstream accuracy.
